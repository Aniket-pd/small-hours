The [contest-specific rules](https://dev.to/page/hacktoberfest-week1-2026-10-05-contest-rules) require a new project **and repository**, created and completed within this window. A qualifying entry needs a published DEV post using the supplied template and challenge tag, a demo (a deployed link **or video**), a code repository link, and an explanation of why open innovation matters. Existing-project pull requests do not qualify. Only the first entry per participant is judged.

The project must put open-source AI at its core and fit the outdoor theme. The rules describe judged awards: one $250 overall prize and six $200 featured-category prizes; only one prize per participant. Awards remain contingent on eligible entry and judging.

## Theme, AI assistance, and optional partners

The [challenge page](https://dev.to/challenges/hacktoberfest-week1-2026-10-05) accepts open-weight models, open-source frameworks, and local inference that materially enable an outdoor experience. Writing quality has the greatest judging weight, alongside relevance, creativity, execution, and applicable partner use. AI assistance is explicitly allowed. Nontrivial reused work must be credited; post-deadline commits must be noted in the README. Prize entries must be in English. The template supplies `devchallenge` and `hf26challenge` tags.

Partner technology is optional. SerpApi has a $100 partner category; eligibility requires meaningful use. A disabled adapter is **not** meaningful live use and is not a basis for claiming that category. The prototype leaves it disabled. Agent-session sharing is optional. The examined pages do not separately mandate blanket AI-tool disclosure; this project nevertheless requires honest disclosure in [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md).
